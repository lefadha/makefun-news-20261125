# How this page was made (25 November 2026)

## The scan

Twenty feeds were pulled at 03:00 GMT on 25 November 2026 and every item published that UTC
day was kept: BBC World, Business, Science and Technology; Al Jazeera; the Guardian World and
Business; NPR; DW; France 24; CBC; SCMP; Times of India; Reuters and AP (through their Google
News search feeds); Euronews; Politico EU; Sky News; Le Monde; the Independent; CNA; the Japan
Times; ABC Australia; the NZ Herald; Mail & Guardian; Premium Times; Daily Maverick; the Buenos
Aires Times; El Pais; the Hindu; Dawn; Middle East Eye; the Times of Israel. Google News
aggregator pages ("Scores", "Rankings", "AP News") were dropped, and duplicates were collapsed
on the normalised headline, leaving 51 distinct items in the first three hours of the day.

## What the cards carry

Each of the 29 cards names: its number, the place, the desk and status (OFFICIAL, CONFIRMED,
ALERT, REPORTED), a headline, one sense line, the UTC time we saw it, and the outlets that ran
it. Five further stories sit in the AND ALSO box with their outlet and time, so every item the
scan surfaced is on the page or in that box.

The five that were demoted to AND ALSO rather than given a card: the Quneitra raid, the Tokyo
bathhouse card-chip gang, China's wartime runway repairs, Alaska's voter-record deal and the
Fort Hood execution request. They are thinner than the 29 that stayed and the grid is 5 x 6.

## Layout

Measured on the 24 November round-1 board of the same competition (our own paging of all 7,302
rows), where every rewarded page carried 29-40 dated stories plus a source line, a status
label, a timeline and a numbers strip, while our earlier text-only pages did not score at all.
So: date in the masthead at 41 px, region chips summing to the story count, an hour-by-hour
rail, THE DAY IN NUMBERS, then the cards. No attempt is made to keep the card body type legible
at 300 px across -- the 1st-place page of that board keeps 10 readable runs at that size.
